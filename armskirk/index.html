Armskirk¶

  • Type: Free city and current capital of the Republic of Leonia
  • Culture: Mercantile, theatrical, civic-minded, and loudly opinionated
  • Population: About 165,000 permanent residents; more than 200,000 during major Trials seasons
  • Founded: Roughly 430 years ago as a fortified ferry-market and coastal trade settlement
  • Government seat: Hall of Accord
  • Capital term: Year 17 of Armskirk's current twenty-year term
  • Notable district: Xiaolong, the “Little Dragon”
  • Known for: The Trials, public debate, markets, rain, rail traffic, and professionally delivered gossip

Armskirk is the current capital of Leonia, though it does not own the title. The capital rotates among Armskirk, Torath, and Corvos every twenty years. Armskirk therefore treats national government like a very long festival: profitable, prestigious, exhausting, and eventually someone else's problem.

The city is a mixture of grand civic halls, rain-dark stone, busy markets, theaters, old bridges, rail yards, lantern courts, and neighborhoods built by generations of arrivals from across Suvaria. Blue-and-gold lion banners hang over the broad avenues. Beneath them, merchants sell food, newspapers shout about politics, and citizens explain—with diagrams—how they could run the republic better.

The Capital at Work¶

The Hall of Accord is both Leonia's government complex and its grandest political stage. Public debates take place in its amphitheater before the Audience, while the Barker Circle, Jugglers, Stagehands, clerks, guards, and archivists keep the republic moving in the rooms behind it.

Outside lies Audience Plaza, where speeches, protests, verdicts, festival announcements, and extremely confident arguments all compete for space. Nearby, the Charter Steps are carved with famous lines from Leonia's founding documents. Politicians use them for speeches. Hecklers use them for elevation.

The Lion Line Central Depot links Armskirk with the wider republic through Leonia's joint rail venture with Tinkertown. Its schedules are clear, comprehensive, and generally accurate until a passenger begins relying on them.

Districts and Landmarks¶

For practical navigation, imagine Armskirk arranged around the Grand Promenade and Hall of Accord at its civic center. The Old Harbor and oldest streets lie northwest along the coast. The Lion Line depot, freight yards, and heavy trade district spread west and southwest. Xiaolong follows the eastern and southeastern canals. The entertainment district—including the Pink Pony—sits between the Promenade and the waterfront, where respectable visitors can reach it conveniently and deny doing so afterward. Hospital Hill rises north of the Promenade, while Candlewick Row and other older working neighborhoods fill the less polished streets around the civic center.

  • The Grand Promenade is Armskirk dressed for company: hotels, theaters, flowered balconies, luxury shops, and streets polished by rain and municipal optimism.
  • Xiaolong, the “Little Dragon,” is one of the city's oldest and liveliest neighborhoods. Founded by merchants, sailors, craftspeople, and laborers from Mian Lung, it is full of lanterns, tea houses, bathhouses, herbalists, shrines, martial schools, workshops, and crowded markets. Locals say, “If you can't find it in Xiaolong, it probably doesn't exist.” They usually say this while trying to sell it to you.
  • The Lantern Courts host weddings, poetry nights, civic receptions, and arguments that began as poetry nights.
  • Lovers' Lookout offers a beautiful view over the city's roofs, bridges, and harbor lights. Couples leave ribbons and promises there; ravens provide unsolicited supervision.
  • The Grand Library of Armskirk holds legal archives, old newspapers, maps, and librarians who consider loud conversation a form of treason.
  • Saint Dacius Civic Hospital crowns Hospital Hill with marble steps, stained glass, skilled healers, and enough charity galas to make suffering feel briefly well-funded.
  • The Trial Champions' Gallery displays famous competitors' weapons, costumes, and portraits. Some champions appear to have defeated perspective itself.
  • The Old Sewer Museum explains the proud history of municipal drainage. Its gift shop sells tiny plungers, proving civilization was worth the effort.
  • Putty Nutty Caverns, a Wetwood's Brokeback Outfitters Family Adventure just beyond the city, offers soft clay passages, glittering mineral pockets, natural slides, souvenir helmets, and the coveted “I Got Putty Nutty!” completion ribbon. It has absolutely no connection to the thoroughly discredited rumor that a visitor died there. The tour company won the court case, and courts are famously never wrong.

A Few Useful Establishments¶

These are places to visit, not complete inventories. Ask at the counter if you need something specific—and accept that the counter may ask something specific in return.

  • Borb's Buns — Owned and operated by Borb and Marda Bunbury with the enthusiastic interference of their family. The shop is narrow, crooked, permanently steaming, and popular with workers, guards, students, and anyone willing to accept that “emotional fillings” are nonrefundable. Do not ask Borb for ketchup. Nol is not allowed near the spice shelf, which is why Nol is usually near the spice shelf.

  • Longzi Wanwu Pu (Dragon Child Toy Shop) — Owned by Madam Lian Zhu, assisted by her grandson Bo Zhu and a fugitive clockwork cricket named Master Click-Clack. The toys are charming, mildly magical, and only occasionally better organized than the customers. Store policy: touch gently, pay honestly, and do not duel the puppets unless you intend to lose.

  • Kardarsha's Crown Cheeses — A traveling cheese wagon run by Kardarsha the Khajiit from Moya I, an old party vehicle enjoying an unexpected second career. Kardarsha sells ordinary cheese, unusual cheese, and cheese whose legal status depends on how one defines “awake.”

  • Iron Ledger Exchange — One of Armskirk's best-known financial houses, handling loans, letters of credit, contract escrow, appraisals, and debt restructuring under managing director Sterling Worth. Its contracts are admirably legible, extremely comprehensive, and somehow heavier than ordinary paper.

  • The Pink Pony Inn — A bardcore tavern where spaghetti-western drama meets gay furry cabaret. Owner Madam Maeve Mane supplies calm authority, while bison-shifter bartender Butch Glassidy supplies drinks with plausible deniability. Expect lute remixes, dramatic performers, private booths, and a strict prohibition against biting without consent.

  • Wetwood's Brokeback Outfitters — Clint Wetwood, “the Otter with No Shame,” runs guided high-country trips from Western Livery Row and takes after-hours bookings at the Pink Pony. Packages include sturdy horses, matching chaps, sunset ram-watching, secluded cabins, the One Bedroll Discount, and family spelunking at Putty Nutty Caverns. The company slogan is: “Above Ground, Underground, and Legally in the Clear.”

  • Yunjing Lifang (Cloud Mirror Salon) — Former theater stylist Farrah Faucet provides haircuts, hot-towel shaves, braids, fur brushing, feather oiling, horn polishing, and emergency repairs after helmets. She can tell whether you need “adventure,” “apology,” or “court appearance” before you sit down.
  • Lianhua Tangquan (Lotus Hot Springs) — Madam Yu Zhen runs this affordable public bathhouse, offering mineral baths, scrubs, massage, laundry, and complimentary gossip under towel rules enforced more strictly than several national laws.
  • Yuzheng Wenquan (Jade Steam Private Onsen) — Private pools, flowering courtyards, tea service, and staff trained not to notice who entered the next room. Jade Steam closes sharply at sunset and firmly denies offering overnight lodging, spirit arbitration, or any employee with more than four arms. Choose according to budget, alibi, and how literally you take a denial.
  • Baicao Tang (Hundred Herbs Hall) — Doctor Cassia Fen, universally called Auntie Dote, sells affordable herbal washes, combat tonics, monster oils, throwing powders, sleep teas, hangover remedies, and professional advice against drinking all of them at once. Three kettles behind her counter are labeled MEDICINE, POISON, and DEPENDS.
  • Yuemen Lutai (Moon Gate Open-Air Theater) — Opera, comedy, shadow plays, civic satire, and heroic tragedies beneath red lanterns. Tonight's classic is The Last Lantern at Eclipse Ford: seven farewells, one sunrise, Lincohn with an axe, and no refunds for crying.
  • Yinzhen Caiyi (Silver Needle Tailors) — Repairs, formal fittings, festival clothes, armor-friendly alterations, hidden pockets, and emergency replacement of sleeves lost to heroism, fire, or poor judgment.
  • Jiudeng Bolihang (Nine Lantern Import Hall) — Xiaolong's broadest market for silk, porcelain, spices, paper charms, enchanted fans, medicinal roots, rare teas, and questionable “authentic relics.” If something is fake, it is at least beautifully fake.

  • Honglun Chefang (Red Wheel Cartwrights) — A respected Xiaolong yard run by Lu Han, known around the rail yards as “Cullen Bowhandle,” with master wheelwright Axel Grease keeping the wheels round and the proprietor's speeches reasonably brief. They promise “better balance, better luck, and fewer idiots loading it,” although only the first two are covered by warranty.

  • Jinyu Chalou (Golden Carp Tea House) — A canal-side tea house owned by Clementine Pekoe, who remembers everyone's usual order—including first-time customers—and insists this is observation, not sorcery. Many rumors begin over the second pot; the first pot is for discovering which conversation loop everyone brought in with them.

  • Baihe Wuguan (White Crane Dojo) — Xiaolong's respected martial school offers public demonstrations and beginner lessons under Master Shrew Fu, a tiny shrew shifter whose patience is shorter than he is and whose staff has never once been used for walking. Senior instructors Beakatrice Plume and Jean-Clawed Van Ram teach grace, balance, and trousers-defying high kicks.

  • Molong Cishen Guan (Ink Dragon Tattoo House) — Led by Zhen “Calamity” Jian, a former frontier camp tattooist whose needle hand is perfectly steady and whose clients' lives become interesting afterward through no legally demonstrable fault of hers. She specializes in protective charms, family crests, spirit animals, and moonlit ink.

Groups in the Public Eye¶

The Grand Ring Authority¶

The Grand Ring Authority of Armskirk is a semi-official civic organization overseeing trade routes, infrastructure projects, labor agreements, and the sort of disputes that become remarkably polite once enough contracts enter the room. It works publicly with the Stagehands' Syndicate and the Jugglers to keep goods moving and construction standing.

Its leader, Wilsun Fyske, calls himself “the Ringmaster.” In preparation for the Trials, he has funded food distribution, repaired trade infrastructure, and brokered agreements between competing guilds. Supporters call him the man who keeps Armskirk moving. Critics say he does keep the wheels turning, but appears increasingly interested in owning the axle.

Fyske's chief engineer is Victor Von Truss, better known as “the Architect.” Von Truss oversees the Authority's bridges, freight yards, civic works, and exceptionally confident blueprints. Admirers call him a visionary. Contractors call him “sir.” Building inspectors call ahead.

The Authority is also competing in the current Trials, where Fyske argues that Leonia needs efficiency, stability, and considerably fewer committees discussing why the bridge is still broken.

Civic Faces¶

Horace Folio, Grand Librarian¶

Horace Folio runs the Grand Library of Armskirk with the worried concentration of a man who assumes every misplaced page is the opening move of a larger conspiracy. He began his career as a Lion Line timetable clerk. During the rail's early years, he caught a transposed bridge number before it became a very expensive lesson in geography. The city rewarded him by transferring him somewhere the paper was less likely to move.

Horace turned a neglected map room into one of Leonia's best collections of civic surveys, rail schedules, legal notices, and corrected tourist maps. He still carries his old railway pocket watch, set four minutes fast, and insists that punctuality is respect while lateness is “a scheduling opinion unsupported by evidence.” He is helpful to serious researchers, merciless toward folded page corners, and capable of producing the exact newspaper someone needs after only twenty minutes of explaining why they should have asked yesterday.

Sterling Worth, Banker¶

Sterling Worth is the managing director of the Iron Ledger Exchange. He began as a traveling ore-weigher on caravans coming down from the Gundabad Mountains, where he learned that merchants will forgive a bad road long before they forgive a dishonest scale. After a warehouse fire wiped out several small traders, Sterling used his own savings to honor their letters of credit. The decision nearly ruined him, then made his reputation.

Today Sterling arranges loans for merchants, guilds, civic projects, and first-time shopkeepers whose arithmetic survives three cups of tea and one disappointed stare. He carries a set of brass scales he claims are purely ceremonial, although no one has ever seen him stop using them. His favorite advice is, “Never borrow from someone who smiles at the interest rate.” Sterling always smiles immediately afterward.

The Trials in Armskirk¶

Armskirk currently hosts the Trials of the Three Cities, the ritual competition that will choose Leonia's next Ringleader. The city has become equal parts election, carnival, sporting event, trade fair, and rumor with street food.

The party entered as competitors with Kardarsha's help. They survived the opening Super Clash Bros competition and earned a place in the debate phase. The remaining Trials are intentionally secretive, so anyone claiming to know the complete schedule is either lying, running the Trials, or selling tickets.

People the Party Knows¶

  • Ringleader Lincohn Abraham is Leonia's current, term-limited head of government.
  • General Wei Shen, the Master of the Muster, oversees defense, emergency response, and security during the Trials. The former champion sumo wrestler is an old comrade of Black Chao and remains built like a government building with opinions.
  • Kardarsha the Khajiit is the party's local contact, cheese merchant, and walking argument against judging a wizard by his dairy products.
  • Deep Snoat is a discreet broker of rumors and leverage associated with the Pink Pony Inn. Calling Snoat a gossip is like calling a crossbow a pointed suggestion.

Current Party Knowledge: The Frankquill Murder¶

General Wei Shen has privately told the party that Benny Frankquill, Leonia's High Juggler, has been murdered. Frankquill oversaw the republic's treasury, taxes, tariffs, budgets, and trade policy.

The party has only just finished its meeting with General Wei Shen. They have not yet pursued any investigative lead. The murder is not included elsewhere on this page because it is not public knowledge in Armskirk.

Player-safe stopping point: nothing from the unvisited follow-up investigation has been included here.
