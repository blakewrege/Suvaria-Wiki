The Snoat Sheet¶

A Professional's Guide to Publicly Humiliating Your Future Colleagues¶

Your Ring at a Glance¶

  • Grand Ring Authority — Wilsun Fyske: Trade, industry, and the solemn belief that every public problem deserves a privately owned solution.
  • United Assembly — A. Hamil: Competent, prepared, respectable, and capable of turning a sentence into a committee.
  • Veiled Concord — Darkveil: Secretive populists endorsed by the Glow Cloud. All hail.
  • Borb's Buns — Borb: Beloved food, powerful feelings, uncertain sanitation.

Questions They Are Likely to Throw at You¶

  1. Why should Leonia trust outsiders with leadership?
  2. Who pays for your promises?
  3. What will you do when guild profit and public need collide?
  4. How will you keep Armskirk, Torath, and Corvos pulling in the same direction?
  5. Which disaster in your history should the Audience pretend not to have heard about?

Across the Other Ring¶

The Bardy Bunch sing their answers. The Wizened & Confused forget theirs. The Guild of Mild Convenience want standardized business hours. The Protagonists Ascendant all claim to be the chosen one.

Watch the Redcap Resolutes. They are the only joke that is not laughing.

Dirt Deep Snoat Actually Brought¶

Grand Ring Authority — Wilsun Fyske¶

The Valve Shortage That Somehow Lost Three Gold¶

Deep Snoat opens a ledger labeled ABSOLUTELY ORDINARY VALVES.

“Two years ago, Wilsun's companies quietly purchased nearly every spare boiler-pressure valve in Armskirk. Three weeks later, an anonymous safety report declared the city's existing valves dangerously outdated.”

“Bathhouses lost hot water. Bakeries lost steam ovens. The Lion Line pumps began making a noise engineers described as financially ominous.”

“Then an independent company called the People's Emergency Pressure Relief Cooperative appeared with warehouses full of approved replacement valves. Its owner was Wilsun's former foreman, whose mortgage, business loan, and left kneecap were all held by Grand Ring creditors.”

“Wilsun sold him the valves, financed his purchases, charged him warehouse rent, and persuaded the city to impose an Emergency Explosion Prevention Surcharge on every sale. A different Wilsun company collected the inspection fee.”

Snoat turns the ledger sideways. The accounting does not improve.

“The money passed through seven companies. One owns another, which owns a third, which owns the first. After rebates, tariffs, commissions, interest, inspection fees, and something called administrative steam, the records claim Wilsun lost three gold.”

“He purchased a country estate the following week.”

“His accountants are still searching for the profit. One of them has begun questioning whether numbers are legally required to mean things.”

Useful attack: Wilsun created a public emergency, sold the solution through a pressured proxy, financed every stage, and collected the city's response fees. If he cannot find the profit, perhaps the Audience should help by searching his estate.

United Assembly — A. Hamil¶

A Carefully Rehearsed Spontaneous Moment¶

Hamil's famous “unscripted” interruptions have stage directions, scheduled applause, and backup interruptions in case the first interruption is itself interrupted. Snoat also has evidence that Hamil has quietly written speeches for political rivals, allowing the Assembly to prepare both sides of an argument before anyone else learns there are sides.

For presentation, Snoat produces embroidered undergarments marked A. HAMIL, recovered backstage after a late policy rehearsal with a lobbyist in a griffon costume. Hamil insists the garment was a visual aid, the griffon represented infrastructure, and all applause was coincidental.

Useful attack: Ask whether Hamil wants to answer the question now or wait for the cue card instructing the Audience to believe the answer was spontaneous.

Veiled Concord — Darkveil¶

The Dog Park Voting District¶

The Dog Park, which is neither a park nor presently known to contain dogs, is registered as Ward Zero, Precinct Bark. Dogs are prohibited from entering. People are discouraged from entering. Election officials may enter only by proving they are not election officials, at which point they are no longer authorized to enter.

This has not prevented excellent turnout.

  • The precinct lists 108 registered voters, including an empty leash, three invisible angels, a municipal bench that has never been installed, and something described only as “the candidate behind you.”
  • It returned 123 ballots in the last civic election. Officials recounted and certified 124.
  • Most ballots supported the Veiled Concord. Three supported A LOW HUM, one contained a wet pawprint from an animal legally classified as not a dog, and one was completed on the inside of the returning officer's eyelid.
  • Ballots arrive in black envelopes before the election, already sealed and faintly growling. Opening one voids the ballot and occasionally the opener.
  • Poll observers must face away from the precinct and close their eyes. Their reports consistently state that nothing improper was observed, followed by several pages of frantic underlining.
  • Campaigning inside the Dog Park is forbidden. Darkveil denies ever entering it, which is the first statement Snoat finds completely credible.
  • The fence is not visible, not measurable, and fully electrified. This is listed as an accessibility feature.
  • One ballot marked ALL HAIL was counted nine times after the Glow Cloud passed overhead. The election board classified this as weather.

The Concord argues that existence is an unfair residency requirement imposed by traditional parties. Its Dog Park platform includes better lighting for shadows, public drinking fountains that whisper tomorrow's weather, and a strict ban on acknowledging the shapes moving between the nonexistent trees.

Useful attack: Ask Darkveil whether the Dog Park endorsed the Concord—or whether the Concord merely counted everything that growled as consent.

Borb's Buns — Borb Bunbury¶

The Apron¶

Deep Snoat opens a sealed oilskin sack. The smell arrives first: old grease, burnt onions, wet flour, and the lingering ghost of a sausage that died without closure.

Borb's apron was probably white during a more innocent century. It is now layered with gravy stains, flour paste, hardened frosting, and enough rendered fat to repel light rain. Three failed inspection notices are stuck to it permanently. One pocket contains a petrified bun; another appears to be growing chives. The apron is stiff enough to stand unsupported, and when Snoat unfolds it, something inside quietly resigns and crawls away.

Across the chest, beneath several geological layers of dinner, are the words:

KISS THE COOK — IMMUNITY NOT INCLUDED

Snoat places the apron on the table. It remains upright.

“Three inspectors cited this garment. One retired. One became a priest. The third now evaluates buildings because they move less.”

Useful attack: Borb's sanitation is indefensible. Unfortunately, so is making the republic's beloved bun seller look sad on live scrying crystal. Deploy the apron carefully; it may create sympathy, nausea, or lunch orders.

Pocket-Sized Dirt¶

  • Wilsun owns a portrait of himself signing the contract for the portrait. A smaller copy of the portrait appears inside it.
  • Hamil's applause schedule includes a five-second allowance for “authentic public surprise.”
  • A Veiled Concord expense report was submitted in disappearing ink and approved before anyone could read it.
  • Borb stores inspection notices beneath a cashbox labeled NOT BUNS.
  • The party's civic paperwork lists Moya once as transportation, once as property, and once as a dependent. All three forms were approved.

Final Advice¶

The best argument does not always win. The best moment usually does.

Once during the debate, your team may quote or act upon this sheet before a social roll to gain advantage—or use one verified scandal to impose disadvantage upon an opponent's response. One use total. Scandal is a spice, not a meal.

Deep Snoat accepts no responsibility for elections, reprisals, thrown pastries, or facts deployed without adequate theatrical timing.
